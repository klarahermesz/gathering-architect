# Community Design Frameworks

Reference for building communities of practice, learning cohorts, and ongoing groups. Use this file when the user is designing for an ongoing group, not a one-off event.

This skill carries two original tools for actually validating and auditing a community: `assets/community-quick-read.md` for a fast early-stage check, and `assets/community-audit-workshop.md` for a deep dive with a group that's already running. Both work start to finish inside the conversation. What follows is background on the external frameworks the field draws on, useful when someone wants more depth or already knows one of these by name.

## FRAMEWORK 1: COMMUNITY CANVAS

**Source:** https://community-canvas.org
**Developers:** Fabian Pfortmüller, Nico Luchsinger, Sascha Mombartz
**License:** Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0 (free for non-commercial use; check the license before a commercial engagement)
**Published:** 2017

The Community Canvas is a comprehensive design framework for building and diagnosing communities, laid out as a set of themes grouped into sections that a group works through together. It is the broadest of the frameworks named in this file, and it is the one to reach for when someone wants a thorough, structured design conversation rather than a fast check.

The framework itself, its structure, its questions, its guidebook, its worksheets and its Miro templates are all published free at community-canvas.org, along with a rapid-start version of their own. Go there and use their materials. We do not restate them here, and a summary would not serve you well anyway, since the value is in working through their actual questions in their actual wording.

**If you want a self-contained tool from this skill instead:** `assets/community-quick-read.md` for a fast early-stage check, or `assets/community-audit-workshop.md` for an evidence-led audit of a community that already exists. Both run start to finish without any source material.

## FRAMEWORK 2: COMMUNITY WEAVING

**Source:** https://www.community-weaving.org/
**Stewards:** Together Institute (https://www.together-institute.org)
**Creators:** Fabian Pfortmüller, Michel Bachmann, Erin Dixon, Sita Magnuson, with contributions from a large practitioner community
**License:** Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0 (free for non-commercial use; check the license before a commercial engagement)

Community Weaving treats community building as relational work rather than project management, on the view that a community holds together through connection rather than through a plan. It is a good counterweight when a group has become very good at programming and events while the relationships underneath have thinned.

The framework's own patterns, definitions, assessment worksheets and Miro template live at community-weaving.org. Read them there.

**Related:** June Holley's Network Weaver methodology informs its thinking on network structure.

## FRAMEWORK 3: WENGER COMMUNITIES OF PRACTICE

**Source:** Etienne Wenger, *Communities of Practice: Learning, Meaning, and Identity* (Cambridge University Press, 1998); Etienne Wenger, Richard McDermott & William M. Snyder, *Cultivating Communities of Practice: A Guide to Managing Knowledge* (Harvard Business School Press, 2002)
**Theorists:** Etienne Wenger and Jean Lave, the concept originating in their *Situated Learning* (1991)
**Status:** Copyrighted scholarly work, not openly licensed

Communities of practice is the foundational body of theory for groups that build shared expertise through working together over time, and it underpins most professional learning-community and knowledge-management practice you will encounter. If a client says "community of practice" and means it precisely, this is what they are referring to.

This is a body of published scholarship rather than a worksheet, and it does not reduce well. Read the books, or start with the authors' own introduction at wenger-trayner.com. We name it here so a facilitator can recognize the term and know where to go, and for no other purpose.

**Available Resources**
- Official site: https://www.wenger-trayner.com/
- Introduction: https://www.wenger-trayner.com/introduction-to-communities-of-practice/
- The two books above
- Lave & Wenger's situated learning theory and organizational CoP evidence are covered from a research angle in `social-learning-evidence.md`

## FRAMEWORK 4: TACIT COMMUNITY OF PRACTICE MATURITY MODEL (Condensed)

**Source:** https://hellotacit.com/community-of-practice-maturity-model/
**Developer:** Tacit (CoP consulting firm)
**Best For:** Assessing where a CoP currently stands and what needs to develop next.

### What It Is

A maturity model and assessment tool for communities of practice, used to work out where a community currently sits and what it needs to develop next. It is most useful when you already have something running and want a structured way to ask "what does this need in order to grow up?"

The stages, the assessment dimensions and the workshop process are Tacit's own and live on their site. Go there rather than working from a summary: https://hellotacit.com/community-of-practice-maturity-model/

**If you want a self-contained alternative from this skill:** `assets/community-audit-workshop.md` audits a running community from its own evidence, and needs no external material.

### When to Reach for Tacit vs. Other CoP Frameworks

- **Wenger** for foundational understanding of how CoPs work and what cultivation looks like.
- **Tacit** for assessment, progression diagnosis, and roadmap planning.
- **JRC Playbook** for comprehensive enterprise-scale implementation across 8 facets.

### Available Resources

- **Tacit website:** https://hellotacit.com
- **Maturity Model:** https://hellotacit.com/community-of-practice-maturity-model/
- **Tools and templates:** https://hellotacit.com/tools/

## FRAMEWORK 5: JRC COMMUNITIES OF PRACTICE PLAYBOOK (Condensed)

**Source:** https://op.europa.eu/webpub/jrc/communities-of-practice-playbook/en/
**Developer:** European Commission, Joint Research Center (JRC)
**Published:** 2021
**Research Base:** 25+ community manager interviews and surveys across the European Commission
**Best For:** Large organizations, multi-stakeholder CoPs, systematic implementation with strong governance and measurement.

### What It Is

The JRC Playbook is a comprehensive, evidence-based methodology for launching, developing, and sustaining communities of practice inside large organizations. It's particularly strong on governance, facilitation, and measurement.

If your context is a small or informal community, Wenger or Community Canvas will serve better. If you need an enterprise-grade implementation playbook with role definitions, decision rights, and KPI tracking, JRC is the most operational of the CoP frameworks.

### The Eight Success Facets

See the Playbook itself for the full facet-by-facet breakdown: https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/8ea3f1b7-1b3f-11ec-b4fe-01aa75ed71a1



### How to Use It

The Playbook is structured so you can dip in by facet rather than reading linearly. Two common entry points:

- **Launching a new CoP**: Work through Vision → Governance → Leadership → Convening in sequence, then layer in the others.
- **Diagnosing a stuck CoP**: Rate yourselves 1-5 on each of the 8 facets. The lowest score usually reveals the bottleneck.

### When to Reach for JRC vs. Other CoP Frameworks

- **Wenger** for the theoretical foundation and any context where learning-through-practice is the heart of the community.
- **Tacit Maturity Model** for assessment and progression planning.
- **JRC** when you need a comprehensive operational playbook for an enterprise-scale CoP with formal governance and measurement obligations.

### Available Resources

- **Full Playbook (free):** https://op.europa.eu/webpub/jrc/communities-of-practice-playbook/en/
- **Print version and templates:** Available via the JRC publication portal
- Implementation phases, role descriptions, and KPI examples are documented in the source.

## COMPLEMENTARY FRAMEWORKS (Summary)

### FRAMEWORK 6: CMX SPACES MODEL

**Source:** https://www.cmxhub.com/blog/the-spaces-model
**Developer:** David Spinks, CMX (Community Marketing Exchange)
**Status:** CMX's own model, published on their site
**Focus:** Business value and alignment

SPACES is a way of naming which kind of business value a community is actually being asked to deliver, so that the answer becomes a deliberate choice rather than a thing everyone assumed differently. It is the framework to reach for when a community needs to justify its existence to people who hold a budget. Its named pillars are the substance of the model and they are CMX's; read them there.

**Use When:** Need to justify community investment, align with business goals

### FRAMEWORK 7: CHARLES VOGL - ART OF COMMUNITY

**Source:** https://www.charlesvogl.com
**Book:** Charles Vogl, *The Art of Community: Seven Principles for Belonging* (Berrett-Koehler, 2016)
**Status:** Copyrighted book content, not openly licensed

Vogl's work is about belonging rather than logistics: what makes someone feel they are genuinely inside a group rather than merely subscribed to it. It is the reference to reach for when a community has healthy numbers and thin attachment. The seven principles that give the book its structure are its substance, and we do not restate them here. Read the book.

**Use When:** Designing for meaningful, deep belonging

---

### FRAMEWORK 8: FEVERBEE COMMUNITY LIFECYCLE

**Source:** https://www.feverbee.com
**Developer:** Richard Millington
**Status:** FeverBee's own model, published on their site
**Focus:** Understanding growth patterns

Millington's lifecycle work describes a community as moving through recognisable phases, each of which asks something different from whoever is running it. Its usefulness is in explaining why the thing that worked last quarter has stopped working, which is otherwise one of the more demoralising experiences in community building. The stage names and their definitions are his; read them at FeverBee.

**Use When:** Understanding where in growth cycle, planning for transitions

---

### FRAMEWORK 9: GET TOGETHER / PEOPLE & COMPANY

**Source:** https://gettogether.world
**Authors:** Bailey Richardson, Kevin Huynh and Kai Elmer Sotto (People & Company)
**Book:** *Get Together: How to Build a Community With Your People* (2019)
**Status:** Copyrighted book content, not openly licensed

Get Together argues for building a community alongside the people in it rather than on their behalf, and it makes the argument mostly through stories of people who did exactly that. It is the one to hand someone who is trying to run a community single-handed and cannot work out why it keeps needing them. The elements it names and its wording are the authors' own, and the book is short enough to read whole.

**Use When:** Emphasizing member participation and co-creation

## IMPLEMENTATION CHECKLIST

Use this checklist to ensure you're covering the essential elements of good community design:

- [ ] **Vision & Purpose** - Clear, compelling, articulated
- [ ] **Member Profile** - Clear definition of who this is for
- [ ] **Values & Principles** - What guides the community
- [ ] **Shared Activities** - What brings people together regularly
- [ ] **Rituals & Traditions** - Repeated practices with meaning
- [ ] **Roles & Participation** - Multiple pathways visible
- [ ] **Governance** - Clear decision-making processes
- [ ] **Leadership** - Distributed, with succession planning
- [ ] **Communication** - Regular rhythm and multiple channels
- [ ] **Knowledge Management** - Capturing and sharing practice
- [ ] **Relationships & Connections** - Intentionally strengthened
- [ ] **Member Experience** - Welcoming, accessible, inclusive
- [ ] **Measurement** - Tracking health and impact
- [ ] **Sustainability** - Financial model and resource plan
- [ ] **Belonging** - Deep sense of community
- [ ] **Maturity Assessment** - Understanding where we are

## CONCLUSION

Reference notes on ten external community design tools, each named and linked rather than reproduced. In brief:

- **Community Canvas:** Holistic structure
- **Community Weaving:** Relational depth
- **Wenger CoP:** Learning and knowledge
- **Tacit Maturity:** Assessment and progression
- **JRC Playbook:** Operational implementation
- **CMX SPACES:** Business alignment
- **Vogl Principles:** Deep belonging
- **FeverBee:** Growth patterns
- **Get Together:** Member co-creation
- **Canvas MVC:** Rapid validation

**Recommended approach:** Choose 2-3 frameworks that address your primary needs, use them in combination, and adapt to your specific community context.
