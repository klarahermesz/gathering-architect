# The Community Quick Read

A fast, honest conversation for working out whether a community should exist, before anyone spends months building one.

So here is the thing this tool is built around. Communities that work almost always formalize something people were already doing. The ones that fail invent a behaviour and hope people adopt it. Most validation conversations skip straight to design questions and never check which of those two you are holding. This one checks first.

"No" is a legitimate answer here, and a cheap one. Getting it now costs you forty minutes.

---

## How to run it

**Time:** 30 to 40 minutes
**Who:** 3 to 5 people who would actually be involved if this existed
**Format:** Talk it through in order, out loud. One person captures. Do not polish the wording.
**Output:** A yes, a no, or a named design problem. Plus one conversation each person will have this week.

**Working name:** _______________________________________________

**Date:** _______________________________________________

**In the room:** _______________________________________________

Say this out loud before you start: *the job here is to find out whether this should exist, and no is a useful answer.* Groups that skip that line spend forty minutes justifying instead of checking.

---

## Round 1: Is it already happening? (10 minutes)

Each person names one real thing from the last month: two people in this group who helped each other without anyone organizing it. Real names. Real occasion. Something that actually took place.

Not "people would definitely want this." Not "I think there's demand." An instance.

> _Instance 1:_ _______________________________________________

> _Instance 2:_ _______________________________________________

> _Instance 3:_ _______________________________________________

> _Instance 4:_ _______________________________________________

**If you cannot get to three between you, stop and say so out loud.** That is the finding, and it is worth more than the rest of the conversation. It does not always mean no. Sometimes it means the behaviour is real but invisible to this particular group, and your next move is to go and look properly rather than to start building.

> _What we found:_ _______________________________________________

---

## Round 2: What does joining cost? (12 minutes)

Count what it actually costs someone to reach their first useful interaction. Be specific and count in two currencies.

**Minutes:** from deciding to try it, how long until they get something worth having? Include signing up, working out where to post, waiting for a reply.

**Social risk:** what do they have to expose to take part? Asking a question in front of strangers is not free. Admitting you do not know something in front of colleagues is expensive.

Then, in the same sitting, what do they get?

> _Cost in minutes:_ _______________________________________________

> _Cost in social risk:_ _______________________________________________

> _What they get, in that same first sitting:_ _______________________________________________

**If the cost lands before the benefit, you have found your design problem.** This is the most common reason good community ideas quietly die, and it is fixable once you have named it. Usually the fix is to move something valuable to the front, before anyone has to expose anything.

---

## Round 3: Who runs it when you are ill? (10 minutes)

Name the person. Not the role, not "we'd share it," not "the committee." The individual who keeps this going for two weeks if the convener disappears without warning.

> _The name:_ _______________________________________________

> _What they would actually do in those two weeks:_ _______________________________________________

**If there is no name, this is a service that one person delivers.** That is a perfectly good thing to be, and plenty of valuable things are exactly that. But it should be a decision you made rather than something you discover in eight months when that person burns out. Decide now which one you are building.

> _So we are building:_  ☐ a community  ☐ a service one person runs  ☐ not sure yet, and that's the next thing to resolve

---

## Close: one conversation each (5 minutes)

Not an action item. Not a pilot. Not a test. Each person names one specific human being they will talk to this week, and the question they will ask them.

The reason it is a conversation and not an experiment: at this stage you do not yet know enough to design a test worth running, and a badly designed test will give you a confident wrong answer.

## A worked example

Five freelance translators, about forty people in a loose WhatsApp group, one of them doing all the organizing and wondering whether to turn it into something more formal.

**Round 1.** They got four instances easily: someone passing on an overflow client, two people proofreading each other's medical translations, a recommendation for a terminology tool, someone talking a newer translator out of accepting a bad rate. All real, all recent, none organized. Strong signal, and it surprised them how much was already happening.

**Round 2.** Cost in minutes was low, the group already existed. Social risk was the problem. Asking for help with a translation in front of forty competitors means admitting you found something hard, in a market where you are all bidding for the same work. The benefit arrived only after you took that risk. Named design problem: the value has to come before the exposure.

**Round 3.** No name. Long pause. Everything ran through one person, and she had not noticed until asked. The group decided honestly that it was a service she ran, and that the interesting question was whether she wanted to keep running it.

**Close.** Four conversations that week, all with people who had gone quiet in the group, asking one question: what stopped you posting the last time you thought about it?

**What happened.** They did not build a community. They changed the group so that a paid terminology resource and a rate-benchmark document arrived every month without anyone having to ask for anything. Value before exposure. Posting went up on its own, and the bus factor conversation happened properly six months later, with more people willing to take a share.
